Ingredient guide

Skincare words, explained

A quick A to Z of terms and ingredients you will often see on skincare labels.

Emollient

An ingredient that softens and smooths the skin, such as shea butter and squalane.

Fragrance-free

A product with no added perfume. A good choice if your skin is sensitive.

Glycerin

A humectant found in many moisturisers and cleansers. It helps skin hold on to water.

Humectant

An ingredient that attracts water to the skin. Glycerin and hyaluronic acid are common examples.

Hyaluronic acid

A humectant that can hold a lot of water. It is used to give skin a plump, hydrated feel.

Niacinamide

A form of vitamin B3, often used to support the skin barrier and the look of even skin tone.

Occlusive

An ingredient that forms a protective layer on the skin to slow water loss, such as petrolatum and some plant butters and waxes.

Shea butter

A rich plant butter from the nut of the shea tree. It is an emollient that helps soften dry skin.

Squalane

A lightweight, non-greasy emollient that helps skin feel soft.

Vitamin C

An antioxidant often used to help skin look brighter. It can irritate some skin, so introduce it slowly.

General information only. Check your product label for what it actually contains, and see a doctor or dermatologist for ongoing skin concerns.
